# Legal memo: what speed limits San Mateo may set on its surveyed streets

Draft, October 25, 2026. Research notes for the speeds tool, not legal advice.

## Summary

1. **Every proposed increase is optional.** No section of state law requires the 16 increases in the 2026 survey. On each of them, at least one option (keeping the current limit under CVC 22358.8, or an extra 5 mph reduction under 22358.7) would allow the current limit or lower. Each option needs a finding or a records check that the survey data alone can't confirm.
2. **The survey left rounding room unused on 13 segments.** On these segments the city could set a limit 5 mph lower than proposed using rounding alone, under CVC 22358.6(c). That rule needs no further finding. One of the 13 is Pacific Blvd (Concar Dr – S Delaware St), where the survey proposes 25 → 30. With rounding alone the city could keep 25.
3. **W 20th Ave / E 20th Ave applies two reductions the statute doesn't allow together.** The survey sheet rounds 35 down to 30 under 22358.6(c), then cuts another 5 under 22358.6(b). Section 22358.6(c) bars the second step. A 25 limit is still within reach through 22358.7, since the sheet notes a nearby high school. The legal basis on the sheet should be corrected so the 25 limit holds up.
4. **San Mateo can't use speed cameras.** The program covers six named cities (CVC 22425(a)(2)), and no law through 2026 adds San Mateo.
5. **The 2006 traffic calming policy is still the city's guiding document.** It doesn't allow physical speed devices on any surveyed segment as the survey classifies them, because all are arterials or collectors. The policy's volume tests apply only to traffic diversion devices, not to speed devices.

## 1. How California sets speed limits from a survey

**Default limits.** Most residential and business streets have a 25 mph limit set by state law, CVC 22352(b)(1). A city may raise a limit above 25 only on the basis of an engineering and traffic survey (CVC 22357(a)). It may lower a limit, in 5 mph steps down to 15, on the same basis (CVC 22358(a)).

**What a survey must consider** (CVC 627(b)):
- prevailing speeds,
- crash records,
- conditions not readily apparent to a driver.

**What a city may also consider** (CVC 627(c)):
- residential density, defined by counts of dwellings or business structures along a quarter mile,
- the safety of people walking and biking, with added weight for children, seniors, people with disabilities, and unhoused people.

Conditions a driver can readily see, such as width, curves or grade, do not on their own justify a lower limit (CVC 22358.5).

**Why the survey matters for enforcement.** If a limit isn't backed by a current survey, it counts as a "speed trap," and officers can't enforce it with radar or lidar. A survey is current for 7 years, extendable to 14 years if an engineer finds no significant change (CVC 40802(a)(2), (c)(2)(B)). Local streets, school zones, senior zones and business activity districts are exempt. So are limits adopted under 22358.4, 22358.7 or 22358.8 (CVC 40802(a)(2), (c)(2)(B)(ii)).

**Rounding.** The base rule is to round the 85th-percentile speed to the nearest 5 mph (CVC 22358.6(a); CA MUTCD 2026 §2B.21 ¶12z). From there:
- **When the nearest step is up or equal** (for example, an 85th of 33 rounds up to 35), the city may instead round down to the step below. That would be 30 here. No other finding is needed (22358.6(c); ¶12ab). After this, no further cut under (b) is allowed (22358.6(c); ¶12ad).
- **When the nearest step is down** (for example, 37 rounds down to 35), the city may cut another 5 mph. The survey must document written reasons consistent with CVC 627 and 22358.5, approved by a registered engineer (22358.6(b); ¶12aa, ¶12ac).
- **An 85th exactly on a step**, such as 45.0, is treated like the round-up case (CA MUTCD 2026 Table 2B-103(CA)). The survey follows the same convention.
- **Total reduction** below the 85th percentile may not exceed 12.4 mph (22358.6(e); ¶12ae).
- **Any other lowering is prohibited** except under 22358.7 (¶12ag).

**Which edition governs.** CA MUTCD 2026 took effect January 18, 2026, replacing the 2014 edition, Revision 9 (Caltrans/CTCDC Item 26-01). The speed-zoning rules moved from §2B.13 to §2B.21. San Mateo's field counts ran March 10 to July 17, 2026, and each sheet certifies compliance with "the current revision" of the CA MUTCD.

**Amendments checked through October 2026:**
- **AB 1014** (2025, ch. 287) amended 22358.6–22358.9, effective January 1, 2026. Among other changes, 22358.6(f) now says the city may keep its current limit, or restore the prior one, "without further reduction."
- **AB 382** (2025, ch. 555) amended 22358.4, the school zone limits.
- **No 2026 bill** changing 22357–22358.9 or 40802 was found.

The legislature's own site blocks automated access, so these texts are from the California.Public.Law mirror, marked "updated Jan. 1, 2026," and should be spot-checked on leginfo before publishing.

## 2. Options AB 43 gives the city, and who qualifies

| Option | Section | What it allows | Criteria | Checkable from the survey data? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Round down | 22358.6(c) | One step below the nearest 5 mph step, when the nearest step is up or equal | None beyond the survey | Yes |
| Documented 5 mph cut | 22358.6(b) | 5 below the nearest step, when the nearest step is down | Written reasons in the survey, signed by an engineer, under CVC 627 and 22358.5 | Only where the sheet already applies it (27 sheets) |
| Extra 5 mph | 22358.7 | 5 below the limit reached through 22358.6 | (1) A city finding that the limit is still more than is reasonable or safe, **and** (2) either (a) or (b) below | No; see "needs" in the tables |
| Keep current, or restore prior | 22358.8 | The current limit, or the prior limit (no more than 5 below current), without further reduction (22358.6(f)) | The limit was set by a survey, **and** an engineer finds no general-purpose lanes added since. Not for freeways. | No: needs the city's records and an engineer's finding |
| Business activity district | 22358.9 | 25 or 20, enforceable without a survey | See (c) below | Partly: lanes and parking from the sheets; frontage, zoning and spacing need a check |

The two ways to qualify for the extra 5 mph under 22358.7:
- **(a) Safety corridor.**
  - *Statute* (22358.7(a)(1)): the segment has been designated a safety corridor, and no more than one-fifth of the city's streets may be designated. The statute leaves the definition to Caltrans (22358.7(b)(1)).
  - *CA MUTCD* (¶12ai–12ar): a safety corridor is a segment "where the highest number of serious injury and fatality crashes occur," for all modes, chosen with crash weighting factors, and an engineer must sign off on the end points. Together with the one-fifth cap, that makes safety corridors roughly the city's worst fifth of streets for deaths and serious injuries.
- **(b) Walking and biking generator.**
  - *Statute* (22358.7(a)(2)): the segment is adjacent to land or a facility that generates high concentrations of bicyclists or pedestrians. The statute leaves the definition to Caltrans (22358.7(b)(2)).
  - *CA MUTCD* (¶12at–12au): there must be a generator from Table 2B-105(CA), such as a school, park, transit stop, retail or sidewalks, within 1,320 ft. The segment must also rank in the city's top 20% for pedestrian and bicyclist deaths and serious injuries over 3–5 years. This crash test comes from the manual, not from the statute.

**(c) Business activity district criteria** (22358.9):
- a downtown, urban village or commercial zoning,
- at most four lanes,
- adjacent limits of at most 30 (to set 25) or at most 25 (to set 20),
- at least three of these four: half or more of the frontage is retail or dining; parking along the street; signals or stop signs at most 600 ft apart; uncontrolled marked crosswalks.

It can't be combined with 22358.7 or 22358.8 (22358.9(c)).

**For all three newer options** (22358.7, 22358.8, 22358.9), officers may issue only warnings for the first 30 days of a lower limit when the driver is 10 mph or less over.

**A correction to the project brief and my earlier note.** Under current numbering:
- the extra 5 mph is **22358.7**,
- keeping or restoring a prior limit is **22358.8**,
- business activity districts are **22358.9**.

The brief listed business districts as 22358.8, and my Stage 0 note listed them as 22358.7. Both were wrong.

## 4. Speed cameras

**San Mateo can't use them.** CVC 22425 lets only "designated jurisdictions" run speed safety systems: Los Angeles, San Jose, Oakland, Glendale, Long Beach and San Francisco (22425(a)(2)). The program ends January 1, 2032 (22431).

**Later laws haven't changed this:**
- **SB 1279** (2026, ch. 689) requires image blurring, and lets Long Beach add two systems on Pacific Coast Highway.
- **AB 289** (2025) covers state highway work zones.
- **SB 720** (2025) concerns red-light cameras.

None of these adds San Mateo. Using speed cameras on city streets would take new state legislation.

## 5. The 2006 Neighborhood Traffic Calming Policy

**Still in force.** The policy is the city's "Neighborhood Traffic Management Program," adopted October 16, 2006 (DocumentCenter/View/1211). Two current city pages link to it:
- "Speed Limit Establishment" (cityofsanmateo.org/2099), as the "Neighborhood Traffic Management Plan."
- "City Staff Evaluation Process" (cityofsanmateo.org/3329), which calls it "the primary guiding document to address speeding."

No replacement policy was found. The old program page (cityofsanmateo.org/2122) now returns "not found."

**What it says about physical ("Step 2") devices** (policy pp. 12–14):
- **Ineligible streets.** Not allowed on streets that are General Plan collectors or arterials, emergency response routes, bus routes or truck routes.
- **Speed test.** Speed devices (speed cushions, traffic circles, chokers, raised intersections) need an 85th-percentile speed "greater than 7 miles per hour over the posted speed limit." The same page also mentions a 32 mph threshold. The city's 3329 page states the test as average prevailing speed more than 7 mph over the posted limit.
- **Volume test.** The volume test (more than 1,000 vehicles a day, with at least 25% cut-through) applies only to diversion devices, such as turn restrictions, diverters and median islands. The policy says either test may be used (p. 16). This corrects the brief: speed devices don't need the volume test.
- **Other requirements.** Majority support among residents who respond to the city's survey, a suitable device location, and diversion within the Portland impact curve.
- **Step 1 measures** (radar signs, striping, signs) are allowed on any street.

**Applied to the survey.**
- **Classification.** The survey classifies 74 segments as minor arterials and 47 as major collectors. The other 2 descriptions weren't parsed, but all segments are larger streets by the survey's own scope.
- **Result.** As the survey classifies them, no surveyed segment can get Step 2 devices.
- **Caveat.** The policy goes by the General Plan's own classification (Circulation Element, Figure C-1, 2011, or its General Plan 2040 successor), which may differ. Checking each segment against that map is a Stage 3 task.
- **Speed test.** 16 of the 123 segments pass the more-than-7-mph test.
